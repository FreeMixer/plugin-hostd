# Chains: instances run in order in one worker, a crashed member quarantined

Status: ruled 2026-10-19 (coordinator decision log 00:55, omx-clap-core#104), built for plugin-hostd#101. Floor of
one member and LV2 chains under `require_pins` ruled 2026-10-21, built for plugin-hostd#131.

## Why

omx-clap-host gives every plugin its own JACK client, and every client is a node of the graph with its own wake-up per
cycle. Most of a cycle's time goes on those wake-ups, not on the plugins (omx-clap-core#104 has the numbers). A strip
that runs four plugins in series wakes four nodes. omx-clap-host can run a chain of plugins in one client and call them
in order inside one process callback. To do that it must be told which instances form a chain, and in what order, and
the daemon must put those instances in one worker. A chain shares one process, so one member's crash takes the others
down. plugin-hostd exists to isolate crashes, so the member that crashed must leave the chain and the rest go on.

## The verbs

    chain <chain> <instance> [<instance> ...]                resp 0
    chain_clear <chain>                                      resp 0

`<chain>` is a number, as an instance is. `chain` declares the chain's members in order, and they must be instances an
`add` made. The chain gets one worker, of place `chain:<chain>` in `worker_list`. Each member leaves the worker it was
in: its state is saved first, its `remove` goes to that worker, and the new worker replays it like a respawn. The
add, the state, the verb ledger and the connections come back in that order. Declaring a chain that is already
declared gives it the new order; a member left out of the new order goes back to a worker of its own. `chain_clear`
gives every member a worker of its own and forgets the chain.

A refusal changes nothing:

| code | when |
|---|---|
| `CHAIN_UNKNOWN_INSTANCE` | a member that no `add` made |
| `CHAIN_DUPLICATE` | the same instance twice in the list |
| `CHAIN_INSTANCE_BUSY` | a member already in another chain |
| `CHAIN_MIXED_FORMAT` | members that one worker program does not run together (a format of `worker_list` each) |
| `PLACEMENT_INVALID` | a member whose plugin may not share a worker while `require_pins` is on (see below) |
| `NO_SUCH_CHAIN` | `chain_clear` of a chain no `chain` declared |

A chain of one member is valid: the fewest members is the declared constant `chain_members_min`, 1, so the trip of
one isolated plugin is a chain of one and maps like any other. A list of fewer members, or a word that is not a number,
is outside the grammar (-902). The draft's floor of two is overruled (2026-10-21).

## What the worker is told

A worker that runs chains in one client (omx-clap-host, for the `clap` and `lv2-clap` rows) is sent the same `chain`
line after the adds of its replay. That line carries the members it holds, in order. A worker that does not run
chains (mod-host) holds the members in one process and its connections give the order; it is never sent the line. A
worker's refusal of the line is written to stderr and is no failure. The members still share the worker, one client
each, as an older omx-clap-host runs them.

Bypass, parameters and every instance verb still name one instance by its own number. The daemon routes them to the
chain's worker like any other verb. The protocol has no latency verb; a latency the worker reports stays the plugin's.

## A crash

A chain's worker is a pool to the supervisor, and it attributes a death the same way:

- When the death names a culprit (the verb in flight, or the suspect inside `suspect_window_ms`), the culprit is
  quarantined (`instance_quarantined <instance> <worker>`) and moved to a worker of its own. The chain's worker is
  respawned with the other members in their order. When their replay is done, `chain_formed <chain> <worker>
  <instance>,...` names the order that runs now.
- When it names none, the members are split, each into a worker of its own, as a pool's are. The member that dies
  again on its own is the culprit. It is quarantined, and the others rejoin the chain in their order.

A quarantined member keeps its place in the declared order. `quarantine_clear` of it puts it back in the chain, and
the chain's worker is formed again with every member that is not quarantined. `remove` of a member takes it out of the
chain, and the chain's worker is sent the order without it. A chain whose last member is removed is forgotten.

## Not here

What omx-clap-host does with the `chain` line, one client and one callback, is omx-clap-core#104. A `chain:` place
cannot be named by `add`: an instance joins a chain only through `chain`.

## A chain under require_pins

A pool's world holds the whole search path, so a plugin whose pin holds only in a narrowed world may not share a
worker while `require_pins` is on. Every LV2 plugin is one (`pinned_own_only`, on the `lv2` row and on the `lv2-clap`
row, omx-clap-host's adapter): its pin holds only in a world that holds no bundle but the pinned ones.

A chain's worker is not a pool in this: its members are known before it starts. The format's `narrow_world` takes the
list of the uris the worker runs, and gives the worker a world of one link per bundle that holds one of them: the
union of the members' bundles. Each member's bundle is pinned, so every bundle the world holds is a pinned one, and
each member's pin holds in it. An own worker is the list of one. The world is made again on every start of the worker,
so a member that leaves (quarantined, split, removed, left out of a new order) takes its bundle out of it.

So, while `require_pins` is on, a chain refuses with `PLACEMENT_INVALID` only:

- a member whose format pins it to a world of its own and cannot narrow a world to a list (no `narrow_world`);
- under `pin_mode first-use`, a member whose pin does not say `verdict=in-process`, as for a pool.

A pool is unchanged: its members come and go by `add`, so its world is the whole search path, and an LV2 plugin
placed in one is still refused. omx-clap-core#104's bench is eight LV2 plugins through the adapter as one chain.
